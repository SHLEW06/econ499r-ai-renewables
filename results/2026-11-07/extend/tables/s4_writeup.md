**AI isn't the national trend.** With data-center jobs, the renewable-share coefficient barely moves across the three models: -0.012 with state FE only (p = 0.046), -0.011 with measured drivers (p = 0.071) and -0.010 with two-way FE (p = 0.100). Leaving out year effects doesn't make it positive, so the national rise isn't loading on AI. **What the drivers show (interim: wind cost and the PTC pending).** The year effects rise steadily for all three outcomes. A path fitted from measured drivers that vary by state, namely state RPS/CES targets (LBNL), solar resource x utility-scale solar LCOE (LBNL Utility-Scale Solar), solar resource x the solar ITC (P.L. 117-169, PDF pp. 98 and 103) and 2015 gas share x the Henry Hub price (EIA), correlates with them at 0.81 (renewable share), 0.84 (ln wind) and 0.76 (ln solar) over 11 years. That is consistent with these drivers' role but doesn't prove it: any series that rises over 2015-2025 correlates with a national trend. Solar LCOE fell from 121 to 51 $/MWh (2022) and rose to 61; the ITC was 30%, 26% for 2020-2021, and 30% again under the IRA. One exception: postings and ln solar generation are positive without year effects (+0.123, p = 0.007) and with drivers (+0.159, p = 0.003), and gone with year effects (-0.046, p = 0.565): a 2019-2025 trend the measured drivers don't capture. All are associations.
