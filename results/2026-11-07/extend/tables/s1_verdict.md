**Verdict.** With Fang's Table A1 (supplementary material), our data match Fang's for ln AI postings, EP, POP and EDU to within 0.01 SD, including min and max, and the same N (204). GDP is 0.090 higher, consistent with chained 2017 vs 2012 dollars. Two rows are flagged. **EPU** is 0.708 lower than Fang's (1.19 SD), close to ln 2, which is consistent with Fang's index being about double ours; with state fixed effects in logs, a constant multiple doesn't change any coefficient (the earlier fallback, means implied by Table 2's constants, missed this gap). **Renewables** on current SEDS are 0.32 SD below Fang's mean, while SEDS as published in June 2023 is 0.04 SD off, consistent with Fang having used the pre-October-2023 basis. **SEDS as published in June 2023 largely reproduces Fang:** the same sign, a similar size in columns 1-2 (+0.0346 and +0.0435 against +0.0428 and +0.0452), and about two-thirds of Fang's size in column 6 (+0.0348, p = 0.057, against +0.0531). Of the move from current to June 2023 SEDS, 61% (column 6, postings) comes from the accounting change and the rest from EIA's later data revisions. Supplement B1's negative indicators run on the same samples (N = 192, 204, 204): Fang's coal (-0.178) and gas (+0.0023) coefficients fall inside our CIs (-0.119 and +0.0160), petroleum doesn't (+0.0067 against -0.0160), and none is significant in either. But Fang's spec has no year effects, and on the June 2023 basis a linear trend turns column 6 to -0.0419 (p = 0.121), while pet-care employment, which has nothing to do with energy, is associated with renewables more strongly than AI (+0.2980, p = 0.003; distilleries +0.1003, p = 0.040). In our final spec, the June 2023 basis gives -0.0211 (p = 0.635; 2019-2021, the years the release covers) for postings. Year effects remove national trends but not state-specific ones: distillery employment stays significant in our spec even with state linear trends (+0.0675, p = 0.040).
